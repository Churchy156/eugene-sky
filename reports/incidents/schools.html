🏫🏈 School days & high-school games vs crashes & police calls

Updated Oct 28, 2026 08:44 AM PT. All dates are Pacific Time calendar days. 9 districts: Eugene 4J, Bethel, Springfield, Junction City, Monroe, Harrisburg, Creswell, Pleasant Hill, South Lane (Cottage Grove). 13 high schools.

Short answer: Lane County has more crashes on school days than on weekdays when schools are off — about 11% more, even after matching the same weekday, month, year, holidays and weather, and even leaving out the Christmas–New Year break. Injury crashes go up too, by less. Police calls don't change with school days. High-school football nights looked like they had more injury crashes, but that turned out to be a calendar effect (the Fridays with no game are mostly in late November and Black Friday) — it disappears when those are left out. We ran 44 comparisons; 9 held up after correcting for running so many tests (14 looked interesting on their own).

What we found

  • School day vs weekday off (Eugene 4J): Lane crashes +11% (range +7% to +15%) — holds up; injury & deadly crashes +6% (range +3% to +9%) — holds up; Eugene police calls +2% (range -1% to +5%) — not reliable.
  • Same thing, leaving out Christmas–New Year: Lane crashes +10% (range +5% to +14%) — holds up.
  • Each extra district in class (0–9): Lane crashes +1.6% (range +1.1% to +2.0%) — holds up.
  • Single days off (teacher/in-service days, one-day holidays) vs school days: Lane crashes -1% (range -10% to +8%) — not reliable; Eugene police calls -10% (range -18% to -2%) — not reliable.
  • Early-release days vs normal school days: Lane crashes -2% (range -6% to +3%) — not reliable; injury crashes -3% (range -6% to +1%) — not reliable.
  • HS football Friday vs other fall Fridays: injury & deadly crashes +18% (range +5% to +34%) — holds up — but Sep 1–Nov 15 only: +4% (range -18% to +31%) — not reliable, and more games on a Friday doesn't mean more crashes (+0.7% (range -1.0% to +2.5%) — not reliable per game). Too few no-game Fridays in the police-call years to test.
  • Each extra HS home game (any sport): injury crashes +0.5% (range +0.2% to +0.7%) — holds up per game — small, and most likely just "school-season days are busier".
  • Big rivalry games (e.g. Thurston–Springfield, Sheldon–South Eugene): Lane crashes +4% (range +1% to +7%) — not reliable; locally hosted playoff games: injury crashes +4% (range +0% to +8%) — not reliable.

How this was done

Outcomes: ODOT crashes 2007–2024 (Lane County; injury & deadly crashes in Lane, Linn, Benton, Douglas, Marion, Lincoln) and Eugene/Springfield police calls (Jan 2023–Oct 2026). Quasi-Poisson regression on daily counts. School-day tests use only weekdays in Sep–Jun and control for weekday×month, year, US holidays and weather (rain, low/high temperature, air pressure). Football-Friday tests use only fall Fridays (controls: month, year, holiday, weather). Benjamini–Hochberg correction across all 44 tests ("holds up" = q < 0.05). Full results: school_correlations.csv. Data columns are in the main dataset (schools_in_session_count, school_4j_in_session, districts_out_count, early_release_any, hs_home_games, hs_football_friday, hs_rivalry_game, …).
