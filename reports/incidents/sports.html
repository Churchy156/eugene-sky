🏈🏀 Ducks & Beavers home games: betting lines vs crashes & police calls

Updated Oct 28, 2026 8:17 AM PT. All dates are Pacific Time calendar days.

Short answer: when the Ducks or Beavers lose a home game they were expected to win (an "upset"), or when the result is a big surprise compared with the Vegas betting line, there is no reliable change in crashes or police calls that day or the next. We ran 724 comparisons; none held up after correcting for running so many tests (44 looked interesting on their own, about what luck alone would give: ≈36).

What we found

  • Upsets don't stand out. Lane County crashes on the day of a big surprise: +0% (range -6% to +6%) per 10 points of surprise (football, 178 game days).
  • One hint worth watching: the day after the Ducks lost a home football game they were favored to win, Lane County crashes were +35% (range +4% to +75%) — but that rests on just 10 games and does not survive the many-tests correction, so it is probably luck.
  • Men's basketball: Lane County crashes on days the home team lost when favored: +7% (range -4% to +19%) (71 games) — not clearly different.
  • Civil War days have fewer crashes (-18% (range -29% to -6%), 6 counties), but that game is on Thanksgiving weekend, when traffic is unusual anyway — it's the holiday, not the rivalry.
  • Police-call records only start in 2023, so there are too few football game days to test them; basketball tests showed nothing.

How this was done

Closing betting lines (spread, over/under, moneyline) come from the Sportsbook Reviews Online archive (football 2007–2022, men's basketball 2007–2021) and ESPN's odds feed (median across real sportsbooks) for later years and women's basketball; scores and Top-25 ranks from ESPN. Minor-league baseball and Hayward Field track meets are left out because there are no betting lines for them. Each comparison uses only home game days and adjusts for weekday, month, year, US holidays and weather (rain, temperatures, air pressure); football is also checked using fall Saturdays only. "Probably real" means a Benjamini–Hochberg corrected chance-of-fluke (q) under 0.05. Correlation is not causation.
